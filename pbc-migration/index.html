Both instincts are right. We honor both by realizing "fast vs. slow" is the wrong axis — the hard line is: never one-shot the core onto ~$21M of customer money + SEO in Q4. Inside that line, we go as fast as possible by decoupling speed from the cutover.

1 · Start now

"Sooner" = the start date. Lock the Shopify free promo before end of June, build immediately on a free instance, push to extend the runway to ~Jan 1. Win that and the ~$100k double-pay objection mostly disappears.

2 · Ship expansion stores now

pickleballpaddles.com + PPA/MLP stores are separate instances, zero redirect risk to the $41M core. They bank the conversion/revenue lift early AND become the live proof + dress rehearsal for the core.

3 · Core cutover stays January

Build so thoroughly we're day-one-ready by early December and just waiting on the safe window (as early as Jan 2 if dry runs pass). Speed here is false economy.

NET CALL

Yes — go faster, by starting now and shipping the expansion stores now, not by dragging the core cutover into Q4. Bryce gets sooner-revenue + earlier readiness; Taylor keeps the Q4 guardrail. A botched Q4 cutover is $175k+ direct + permanent SEO loss + customer-balance chaos at peak — the overlap cost is cheap insurance against that, and likely $0 if the promo extends.

🏪 80-location POS

Hardware audit + config + staff training + phased rollout, with a per-store Square fallback on standby. PART 6 · IN-HOUSE VS. OUTSIDE HELP

Taylor's question, answered directly

✅ What AI / Bryce genuinely de-risks → lead in-house

The build: theme, custom code, the 301 redirect map + data-migration scripting, and ongoing management. This is the real saver vs. the 6–8 months / ~$150k quote and the ~$200k agency path.

⚠️ What AI does NOT de-risk → bring targeted help

Financial-liability reconciliation, payment + tax setup, the 80-store POS rollout, and a one-shot live cutover on ~$21M of volume.

RECOMMENDATION

In-house leads; specialists de-risk the one-way doors. Bring outside help for three things only: (1) bandwidth to run a fixed-date live cutover while BAU continues, (2) pattern memory — someone who's done this exact BigCommerce→Shopify migration and carries the gotcha list, and (3) accountability + rollback when real customer money is live. Shopify offers no migration service (points to 3rd-party partners). A scoped QA/migration specialist at ~$15–20k is cheap insurance on customer balances — not a knock on the in-house plan.
